| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| CMAS Ice Diver standard (V2009): overhead environment close to cavern zone 1; thickness measured by secured staff before activities; never in current; rule of thirds; prerequisites (CMAS 2* or equivalent; 50 logged dives, 20 after 2*; dry suit cert + 10 dives if used); minimum 2-day course; 30 m limit; 1:1 under ice; max 50 m line; 30-question test, 80% pass; max 2 dives a day; no-deco recommendation citing cold and circulation; increased DCI risk topic; two independent regulators, no octopus, DIN only, EN250 cold-water, very dry gas, separate first stages for inflators; lamps ≥ 2× dive time; chemical light under 10 m visibility; safety reel ≥ 50 m; two cutting tools; tank minimums (2×7 L or 10 L with two outlets; instructors 2×8 L); instructor minimum age 20 | https://www.cmas.org/document/cmas-hq/standards/ice-diver.html (document "Ice Diving Standards V 2009") |

Physics: Boyle's law, Henry's law, hydrostatic pressure, heat conduction (water ≈ 0.56, air ≈ 0.024 W/m·K near 0 °C, standard physical data), tangent geometry. Example breathing rates are examples, not standards.

## Deepen batch 1 (10 Nov 2026): Chapters 7 to 9

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Gold's formula P = A × h² (P kg, h cm of good clear ice); Table 3 A = 3.5 low risk: 20 cm 1,400 kg, 40 cm 5,600 kg, 50 cm 8,750 kg, 100 cm 35,000 kg; formula not used under 5,000 kg; Table 5: person walking 120 kg needs 10 cm, snowmobile under 500 kg needs 18 cm; white ice about half as strong as blue ice; radial cracks at about half the failure load; circumferential cracks mean breakthrough is near; temperature drop over 20 °C in 24 h, reduce load by 50%; critical speed about 50 km/h for 1 m ice over 15 m water, stresses up about 50%, speed limit 25 km/h | https://www.cdda.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Best-Practice-Ice-Roads.pdf (Ontario guide, based on Alberta's 2013 best practice) |
| Fresh water about 1,000 kg/m³, sea water about 1,025 kg/m³; salt makes water denser | https://www.unols.org/sites/default/files/NOAA2012MTRefresher_Oceanography1.pdf ; https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/ocean/sea-water |
| CMAS skills: hovering motionless at any depth without visual references, bubble check at 3 to 5 m, shut-down drill (pass/fail); separate first stages for dry suit and BC inflators; surface crew per main entry (1 tender, 1 stand-by diver, at least 2 surface personnel, 1 person on shore); tender completely dressed in a dry suit to work at the hole in an emergency, on an isolating wooden plank solidly fixed to the ice; planks 2.0 × 0.4 m × 2 cm; 6 hollow ice screws per main entry for double securing the 2 tender lines and shot line; backup/rescue line for the surface (e.g. breaking through ice); 50 m floating static ropes 8 to 10 mm, tender and rescue in different bright colours; first aid with O2, shelter, warm drinks | https://www.cmas.org/document/cmas-hq/standards/ice-diver.html |
| Ice over flowing water, near streams, bridges and culverts, and near shore often weaker; booming and cracking sounds only mean expansion and contraction; self-rescue (turn back, picks, kick, lie flat and roll); call 911 and throw one end of a rope or a long branch, let go if pulled toward the hole; most people who fall through drown, not hypothermia; about 10 minutes of meaningful movement | https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/survival.html ; https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/ice-safety-gear.html ; https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/index.html |
| 1-10-1: about 1 min cold shock, about 10 min meaningful movement, about 1 h to unconsciousness | https://csbc.ca/1-10-1-principle/ |
| Caveat: 1-10-1 understates the danger; breathing not always controllable; progressive weakening | https://www.coldwatersafety.org/1-10-1-myth |

Illustrative values (labelled on the page): 100 L displaced volume; 90 kg person; boot area 0.06 m²; lying area 0.6 m². Physics: Gold's formula arithmetic, Archimedes' principle, Boyle's law with 1 bar per 10.2 m in fresh water, pressure = force ÷ area.
