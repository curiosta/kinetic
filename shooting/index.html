CH 1

Pellet flight & gravity

Even over 10 m, the pellet is falling the whole way.

Ignoring drag, a pellet crosses the range at constant speed, so its flight time is t = d ÷ v. While it flies, gravity pulls it down by ½ g t². Sights are set (zeroed) for the target distance, so that drop is built into the aim: you never see it, but it's there.

Worked example · 10 m, 0.5 g pellet, no drag (illustrative speeds)
Pellet speedFlight timeGravity dropPellet energy
150 m/s≈ 67 ms≈ 21.8 mm≈ 5.6 J
175 m/s≈ 57 ms≈ 16.0 mm≈ 7.7 J

175 m/s: t = 10 ÷ 175 ≈ 0.0571 s; drop = ½ × 9.81 × 0.0571² ≈ 0.016 m. ISSF rules set 10 m air events at 4.5 mm (.177) calibre.

At 160 m/s, how long does the pellet take to cover 10 m?

CH 2

Recoil & momentum

Push something forward and you get pushed back. A shot is no exception.

Before the shot, the gun and pellet have zero total momentum. Afterwards, the pellet carries momentum m v forward, so the gun must carry the same amount backward: M V = m v. A heavier gun gets the same momentum at a lower speed, which is one reason target rifles are heavy (ISSF caps a 10 m air rifle at 5.5 kg with sights).

Worked example · 0.5 g pellet at 175 m/s
Gun massPellet momentumRecoil speedRecoil energy
5.0 kg (rifle)0.0875 kg·m/s≈ 1.75 cm/s≈ 0.77 mJ
1.0 kg (pistol, illustrative)0.0875 kg·m/s≈ 8.75 cm/s≈ 3.8 mJ

V = 0.0005 × 175 ÷ 5 = 0.0175 m/s. The rifle's recoil energy (½ × 5 × 0.0175²) is 10,000 times smaller than the pellet's 7.7 J, the same ratio as the masses.

Why recoil still matters at 10 m

The pellet is still in the barrel while the gun starts to move, so any movement you add then (a flinch, a grip that changes) goes into the shot. That's why shooters train a position that stays the same through every shot.

The same pellet from a 2.5 kg gun: what recoil speed?

0.0875 ÷ 2.5 = 3.5 cm/s.

CH 3

Angles, wobble & the 10-ring

The rifle 10-ring is a half-millimetre dot. You hit it with angles, not luck.

On the ISSF 10 m air rifle target the 10-ring is a white dot just 0.5 mm across, and each ring is 5 mm wider than the last, out to 45.5 mm. The 10 m air pistol 10-ring is 11.5 mm, out to 155.5 mm. A shot scores the higher ring whenever any part of the pellet hole touches it, so with a 4.5 mm pellet the centre only has to be within 2.5 mm of the middle for a rifle 10, and within 8 mm for a pistol 10.

An aiming error is an angle. At 10 m, 1 milliradian (mrad) = 10 mm on the target.

Worked example · wobble → miss → score at 10 m (from our Hold & Score Lab)
Aiming wobbleOff centreAir rifle ringAir pistol ring
0.2 mrad2 mm1010

Rifle 9-ring: 5.5 mm across, radius 2.75 mm + pellet radius 2.25 mm = 5.0 mm, so a 4 mm miss scores 9. Rifle 10: 0.25 + 2.25 = 2.5 mm, so 2.5 mm ÷ 10 m = 0.25 mrad, about 0.014°.

Decimal scores

With electronic targets, each ring can be split into ten decimal rings (10.0 to 10.9). ISSF uses decimal scoring for 10 m air rifle qualification at its championships; 10 m air pistol is scored in whole rings.

A 0.6 mrad wobble at 10 m: how far off centre, and which rifle ring?

6 mm. The 8-ring reaches 5.25 + 2.25 = 7.5 mm, so 8.

CH 4

Breathing, heartbeat & the hold

Your body is never perfectly still. Shooters learn when it's stillest.

Breathing moves your chest, shoulders and the gun with them. Shooters settle into a natural pause after breathing out and take the shot there. Even then the heartbeat sends small pulses through the body, so the sight picture drifts in a small, slow pattern rather than sitting dead still. Training is about making that pattern as small and as calm as possible.

Worked example · the time you have
ItemNumberWhat it means
ISSF 10 m air rifle qualification60 shots in 75 min75 s per shot on average, including rest
Heart at 60 beats per minute1 beat per secondSeveral beats during a typical aim
A pulse that nudges the gun 0.1 mrad (illustrative)1 mm at 10 m40% of the 2.5 mm rifle-10 window

75 min × 60 ÷ 60 shots = 75 s. 0.1 mrad × 10 m = 1 mm; 1 ÷ 2.5 = 40%.

  • Breathe, settle, aim: take the shot in the natural pause, not while holding your breath to the point of strain.
  • If the hold isn't settling, don't force it. Lower the gun downrange, breathe and start again; there's time (75 s per shot on average).
At 72 beats per minute, how many beats in a 5 s aim?

72 ÷ 60 × 5 = 6 beats.

CH 5

Trigger control & follow-through

The shot isn't over when the trigger breaks. It's over when the pellet has left.

Between the trigger breaking and the pellet leaving the barrel there are a few milliseconds when the gun can still move. A smooth press that doesn't disturb the hold keeps the aim where it was; a jerk rotates the gun just when it matters. Follow-through means keeping your position and aim steady through the shot and a moment after it.

2 mrad/s × 0.010 s = 0.02 mrad; × 10 m = 0.2 mm. Jerk: 0.8 × 10 = 8 mm; the 7-ring reaches 7.75 + 2.25 = 10 mm.

A 0.3 mrad flinch at 10 m: how far off, and what rifle ring?

3 mm. Inside 5.0 mm but outside 2.5 mm, so 9.

CH 6

Range safety, rules & the law

On a well-run range, safety comes from rules that leave nothing to chance.

Even a 10 m pellet carries several joules (Ch 1), enough to injure an eye. ISSF rules (2026 edition) build safety into every step on the range:

  • Safety flags: bright flags go into every rifle and pistol to show it is unloaded; for air guns the flag runs the full length of the barrel.
  • Muzzle discipline: on the firing point, guns always point in a safe direction, and the action is closed only when pointing downrange.
  • Commands: range officials give LOAD, START, STOP and UNLOAD. Guns are loaded only on the firing point after LOAD or START; on STOP, shooting stops immediately.
  • Nobody handles guns while anyone is forward of the firing line.
  • Hearing protection is required near the 25 m, 50 m and 300 m firing lines and on shotgun ranges; shotgun athletes must wear shatterproof eye protection.

The law in India

Firearms licences in India are governed by the Arms Act, 1959 and the Arms Rules, 2016 (Ministry of Home Affairs). The Act refers to target practice by members of rifle clubs or associations recognised by the Central Government. Start at a recognised club or academy, and take any legal question to your club, your state rifle association or the licensing authority. This page does not cover buying, owning or modifying any gun.

Which command must come before anyone loads on the firing point?

LOAD (or START), from the range official.

04 · Governing bodies

Who sets the rules

Sport shooting's rules come from the ISSF; in India the national federation and its state associations run the sport. We checked each on an official website.

Global

World federation

International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF)

Founded in 1907; the world governing body for Olympic shooting, with 159 member federations from 147 countries or territories. Sets the rules, including range safety.

issf-sports.org
Education

ISSF Academy

ISSF's education hub, set up in March 2024 for coach education; ISSF coach licences at C, B and A level follow its courses.

issf-sports.org

India

National federation

National Rifle Association of India (NRAI)

Affiliated to the ISSF and the Asian Shooting Confederation. Runs the national championships, India Open competitions, selection trials and coach and judge courses.

thenrai.in
National range

Dr Karni Singh Shooting Ranges (SAI)

Sports Authority of India's range at Tughlakabad, New Delhi: 72 acres, built to ISSF norms, with 10 m, 25 m and 50 m ranges and six trap and skeet ranges.

sportsauthorityofindia.gov.in
