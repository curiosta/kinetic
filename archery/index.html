CH 1

How a bow stores energy

A bow is a spring you load with your back muscles.

Drawing the string does work: force × distance. If the force rose evenly from zero to the draw weight F over the power stroke s (from brace height to full draw), the stored energy would be E ≈ ½ F s. Real bows store somewhat more because of their force–draw curve. Not all of it reaches the arrow: some stays in the limbs and string as vibration and sound.

40 lb (178 N)25 g≈ 43.5 J≈ 32.6 J≈ 51.1 m/s

40 lb × 4.448 = 177.9 N; power stroke (28 − 8.75) × 0.0254 = 0.489 m; ½ × 177.9 × 0.489 ≈ 43.5 J; × 0.75 ≈ 32.6 J; v = √(2 × 32.6 ÷ 0.020) ≈ 57.1 m/s.

CH 2

Arrow flight & gravity

From the moment it leaves the string, the arrow is falling.

Ignoring drag, an arrow flies at constant horizontal speed and falls under gravity: flight time t = d ÷ v and drop ½ g t² below the line it was launched along. To land on target you launch slightly upward, at θ = ½ asin(g d ÷ v²).

Worked example · 20 g arrow at 57.1 m/s, no drag (from our Arrow Lab)
DistanceFlight timeDrop if shot levelLaunch angle
18 m≈ 0.32 s≈ 0.49 m≈ 1.6°
30 m≈ 0.53 s≈ 1.35 m≈ 2.6°
50 m≈ 0.88 s≈ 3.76 m≈ 4.3°
70 m≈ 1.23 s≈ 7.37 m≈ 6.1°
90 m≈ 1.58 s≈ 12.2 m≈ 7.9°

70 m: t = 70 ÷ 57.1 ≈ 1.23 s; ½ × 9.81 × 1.23² ≈ 7.4 m. World Archery's standard distances are 70 m for recurve and 50 m for compound, with competition distances of up to 90 m.

Why the sight moves

Drop grows with t², so it more than doubles from 30 m to 50 m. Archers set the sight lower for longer distances, which raises the bow arm and the launch angle. Drag slows the arrow along the way, adding a little more drop than this model shows.

What launch angle would 57.1 m/s need at 30 m?

½ asin(9.81 × 30 ÷ 57.1²) ≈ 2.6°.

CH 3

Spine, the archer's paradox & fletching

The arrow bends around the bow and still flies straight. That's no accident.

On release, the string drives the back of the arrow forward while the front sits beside the riser. The shaft bends and oscillates as it clears the bow: the archer's paradox. An arrow with the right stiffness (spine) for the bow's draw weight recovers and flies true; too stiff or too weak and it leaves at an angle.

Fletching keeps the point forward

Vanes or feathers at the back move the centre of drag behind the centre of mass. If the arrow turns sideways, the extra drag on the tail swings it back in line, like a weathervane. Slightly angled vanes also spin the arrow, averaging out small flaws.

Worked example · the same energy, different arrows (40 lb, 28 in)
Arrow massSpeedMomentumDrop if shot level at 70 m
20 g≈ 57.1 m/s≈ 1.14 kg·m/s≈ 7.4 m
25 g≈ 51.1 m/s≈ 1.28 kg·m/s≈ 9.2 m

Same 32.6 J: the heavier arrow is slower but carries more momentum (m × v), which helps it hold its line in wind. 25 g: t = 70 ÷ 51.1 ≈ 1.37 s; drop ≈ 9.2 m.

A 30 g arrow from the same bow (32.6 J): what speed?

v = √(2 × 32.6 ÷ 0.030) ≈ 46.6 m/s.

CH 4

Aiming, angles & scoring

At 70 m, the 10-ring is a tenth of a degree wide.

World Archery's recurve face at 70 m is 122 cm across with a 12.2 cm 10-ring; the compound face at 50 m is 80 cm with an 8 cm 10-ring. The angle a ring covers is angle ≈ size ÷ distance (in radians).

Worked example · how small the 10 really is
DivisionDistance10-ringAngle covered
Recurve70 m12.2 cm≈ 1.74 mrad ≈ 0.10°
Compound50 m8 cm≈ 1.60 mrad ≈ 0.09°

0.122 ÷ 70 ≈ 0.00174 rad. A sight pin 0.8 m from your eye that drifts 1 mm moves the impact about 1 × 70 ÷ 0.8 ≈ 88 mm at 70 m.

The five-colour target

Ten scoring zones: yellow 10 and 9, red 8 and 7, blue 6 and 5, black 4 and 3, white 2 and 1; a miss scores 0. On the compound face the outer four rings are usually removed, leaving yellow, red and blue.

What angle does the compound 10-ring cover, in degrees?

0.08 ÷ 50 = 0.0016 rad × 57.3 ≈ 0.09°.

CH 5

Holding, breathing & let-off

The steadiest bow is held by bones and back, not by arm muscles alone.

A recurve archer holds the full draw weight while aiming. A compound bow's cams give let-off: the holding weight at full draw is only a fraction of the peak. Either way, small movements at the bow become big misses at the target (Ch 4), so archers build a repeatable posture: bow arm extended, drawing with the back, a consistent anchor point and a settled breath.

Worked example · what you hold at full draw
BowPeak draw weightLet-offHolding force
Recurve40 lbnone≈ 178 N
Compound60 lb80%≈ 53 N

20% of 60 lb = 12 lb × 4.448 ≈ 53 N. Let-off values vary by bow; 80% is illustrative.

Breathing and the shot sequence

  • Settle the breath before the final aim: chest movement shifts the bow arm.
  • Hold only as long as it's steady. If you start to shake, let down safely and start again.
  • Clean release: relax the string fingers so the string leaves smoothly, and keep the bow arm still through the follow-through.
A 50 lb compound with 75% let-off: what's the holding weight?

25% of 50 lb = 12.5 lb (about 56 N).

CH 6

Wind, drag & range safety

An arrow is a projectile with real energy. Ranges are designed around that.

A crosswind pushes the arrow sideways for its whole flight. Light arrows are pushed around more by the same force, and slow arrows are exposed for longer, so both drift further. Drag also slows every arrow, so the drop at long range is a little more than the no-drag model.

Worked example · energy an arrow carries
BowArrowSpeedEnergy
30 lb20 g≈ 49.5 m/s≈ 24.5 J
40 lb20 g≈ 57.1 m/s≈ 32.6 J

½ × 0.020 × 57.1² ≈ 32.6 J. Launched at 45°, a no-drag arrow from this bow could fly about v² ÷ g ≈ 330 m: one reason ranges need clear zones well behind the targets.

Range rules

Shoot only at a supervised range. Nock and draw only on the shooting line and only towards the targets. Nobody goes forward of the line until every archer has stopped and the signal is given. Never "dry-fire" a bow (release without an arrow): the energy goes into the limbs and can break them.

Doubling arrow speed multiplies its energy by how much?

E ∝ v², so by 4.

04 · Governing bodies

Who sets the rules

Archery's rules come from World Archery; in India the national federation and its state associations run the sport. We checked each on an official website.

Global

World federation

World Archery

The international federation for the Olympic sport of archery. Sets the rules for target archery (70 m recurve, 50 m compound) and other disciplines.

worldarchery.sport
Education

World Archery coaching & judging

Coaching courses from beginner to elite level and coach-trainer courses; international judge accreditation; beginner and performance badge programmes.

worldarchery.sport

India

National federation

Archery Association of India (AAI)

Founded on 8 August 1973; recognised by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports; affiliated to World Archery, World Archery Asia, the South Asian Archery Federation and the Indian Olympic Association. Oversees recurve, compound, Indian round and para archery.

indianarchery.org
State associations

AAI member units

35 state and UT archery associations plus 9 sports boards and institutions; they host national events such as the 2026 junior nationals (Rajasthan) and senior nationals (Meghalaya).
