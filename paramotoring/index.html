CH 1

Thrust vs drag

A paraglider with a fan on your back: thrust replaces the hill.

A paraglider "spends" height to keep moving (our Paragliding page covers glide in detail). A paramotor, or powered paraglider (PPG), adds a propeller on the pilot's back. In level flight four forces balance: lift = weight and thrust = drag. Since lift ÷ drag is the glide ratio, drag = weight ÷ (L/D). Any thrust beyond that makes you climb.

Worked example · 110 kg all-up, 36 km/h (10 m/s), 400 N thrust (from our Thrust Lab, illustrative)
Wing L/DDragPower to hold heightClimb rate
5 : 1≈ 216 N≈ 2.16 kW≈ 1.7 m/s
6 : 1≈ 180 N≈ 1.80 kW≈ 2.0 m/s
7 : 1≈ 154 N≈ 1.54 kW≈ 2.3 m/s

Weight = 110 × 9.81 = 1,079 N; ÷ 6 ≈ 180 N. Climb = (400 − 180) × 10 ÷ 1,079 ≈ 2.0 m/s. Propellers aren't perfectly efficient, so the engine must make more than this power.

Why thrust falls as you speed up

A static thrust figure (propeller pushing hard while you stand still) is not what you get in flight. In the simple momentum picture, the propeller accelerates a disk of air; as your airspeed rises, the same power adds less extra speed to that stream, so available thrust drops. Climb is hardest just after lift-off, when you still want full thrust and the wing is slow — then improves a little as you accelerate, then falls again as drag rises with speed. Density altitude (hot, high, humid) cuts both propeller thrust and wing lift, so the same field can feel short on a hot afternoon.

Order of magnitude · disk loading (illustrative 1.25 m propeller)
WhatNumberMeaning

ρ ≈ 1.2 kg/m³. The Thrust Lab's 400 N is an all-up push you dial in; real static thrust depends on prop, reduction drive and density. Use the lab to feel how climb collapses when mass rises or L/D falls — not as an engine-dyno sheet.

CH 2

The PPG wing

The same kind of fabric wing, loaded heavier and flown faster.

A PPG wing is a ram-air fabric wing like a paraglider: air enters openings at the front and inflates the cells. With an engine, fuel and frame on your back, the wing carries more weight per square metre. Wing loading (kg per m²) sets how fast the wing flies at trim: in steady flight, lift must equal weight, so speed rises with the square root of the load, v ∝ √(loading).

Worked example · a 22 m² wing (illustrative)
All-up massWing loadingTrim speed (scaled from 36 km/h at 110 kg)
110 kg5.0 kg/m²36 km/h
125 kg≈ 5.7 kg/m²≈ 38.4 km/h

36 × √(125 ÷ 110) ≈ 38.4 km/h. Every wing has a certified weight range; flying outside it changes how the wing behaves.

Certified wings

The Safety Guidelines for Paramotoring and powered parachutes (Version E5) say wings should be EN, LTF, DHV or DGAC certified and recommended for powered flying, with annual airworthiness checks and records kept.

Trimmers, speedbar and reflex

Many PPG wings add trimmers (risers you lengthen or shorten in flight to change the angle of attack) and a speedbar (a foot stirrup that pulls the A-risers forward). Released trimmers and a speedbar let you fly faster for cruise or into wind; pulled-in trimmers slow you for launch and landing. Some PPG wings are reflex: the aerofoil is shaped so that at speed the nose wants to pitch up if the wing is unloaded, which helps resist collapses in rough air — at the cost of different stall and recovery behaviour than a classic free-flight wing. The USPPA's PPG2 syllabus expects pilots to explain speedbar and trimmers, their use, risks and limitations, including for reflex wings. Learn each wing from its manual and from an instructor who knows that model.

Your all-up mass rises by a quarter. Roughly how much faster does the wing trim?

√1.25 ≈ 1.12, so about 12% faster.

CH 3

Thrust line & torque

Spin a propeller one way and it tries to spin you the other.

Newton's third law works on rotation too: the engine turns the propeller, so the propeller pushes back on the engine, and on you, with an equal and opposite torque. Torque = power ÷ angular speed, so a slow-turning propeller at high power gives the biggest twist. It tends to roll and turn the pilot against the propeller's direction; frames often build in offsets and pilots learn to correct for it. Where the push acts matters too: if the thrust line sits above or below the hang points, adding power pitches you forward or back.

Worked example · reaction torque (illustrative motor)
PowerPropeller speedTorque on the pilot
15 kW (full power)3,200 rpm≈ 45 N·m
5 kW (cruise)2,200 rpm≈ 22 N·m

3,200 rpm = 335 rad/s; 15,000 ÷ 335 ≈ 45 N·m: like a 4.5 kg mass hanging on a 1 m lever. Torque is strongest at full power, which is during take-off and climb, close to the ground.

Thrust line and pitch

Reaction torque tries to roll you. A separate effect is pitch: if the thrust line does not pass through the hang points (where the wing holds you), adding power creates a couple. Thrust above the hang points pitches the pilot back (harder to push the wing overhead on launch; a climb attitude that can stall if you also hold brakes); thrust below pitches you forward. Frames, hang-point adjustments and harness geometry are designed to keep that couple manageable — which is why copying a friend's settings onto a different motor can surprise you.

Worked example · pitch couple from a thrust-line offset (illustrative)
Offset of thrust above hang pointsThrustPitch coupleLike…
5 cm400 N20 N·mAbout 2 kg on a 1 m lever, pitching you back
10 cm400 N40 N·mNearly the same size as the 45 N·m roll torque above
10 cm200 N (cruise)20 N·mHalf the thrust, half the couple

Couple = thrust × offset. Real hang-point charts matter more than this toy model; the point is that a few centimetres at full power are not trivial when you are running with a wing overhead.

The USPPA's PPG2 rating (a US reference) requires pilots to understand torque effects and to train on a simulator, a frame hung from a hang point so you can feel the controls on the ground. It also asks them to explain how centre of gravity and wing connection location on the harness affect the machine.

Same 15 kW but the propeller turns at 2,400 rpm. More or less torque?

More: 15,000 ÷ (2,400 × 2π ÷ 60) ≈ 60 N·m.

CH 4

Forward & reverse launch

The wing needs airspeed, not groundspeed. Wind gives you some for free.

To lift off, the wing must reach its flying airspeed. Facing into the wind, groundspeed = airspeed − headwind, so a headwind means a shorter, slower take-off run. In calm air you run and use thrust to build the speed: a forward launch, facing the way you'll fly. In more wind, pilots often use a reverse launch: face the wing to inflate and check it, then turn round and go.

Worked example · wing flies at 32 km/h, acceleration 1.5 m/s² (illustrative)
HeadwindGroundspeed at lift-offTake-off run
0 km/h32 km/h≈ 26 m
10 km/h22 km/h≈ 12 m
20 km/h12 km/h≈ 4 m

Run distance = v² ÷ (2a): (32 ÷ 3.6)² ÷ 3 ≈ 26 m. Halving the groundspeed needed cuts the run to about a quarter. Taking off downwind does the opposite.

The USPPA's PPG1 (student) rating, for example, asks for two forward and two reverse inflations, 30 seconds of kiting the wing overhead, and two unassisted flights, in winds of no more than 8 mph (about 13 km/h).

The wing flies at 32 km/h and there's a 12 km/h headwind. What groundspeed at lift-off?

32 − 12 = 20 km/h.

CH 5

Fuel, endurance & weight-shift

Plan the trip home before you leave, and steer with your hips.

Endurance is usable fuel ÷ burn rate, and range depends on the wind. Into a headwind your groundspeed drops; downwind it rises. The point of no return is how far you can go out and still get back on the fuel you have.

Worked example · 36 km/h airspeed, 15 km/h wind, 2 h of usable fuel after a reserve (illustrative)
ItemNumberHow
Groundspeed into wind21 km/h36 − 15
Groundspeed downwind51 km/h36 + 15
Furthest you can go upwind and return≈ 30 kmR ÷ 21 + R ÷ 51 = 2 h
12 L of petrol≈ 8.9 kgabout 0.74 kg per litre: 8% of a 110 kg all-up mass

Go out upwind and come home downwind: if you go out downwind, the slow, fuel-hungry leg comes last.

Weight-shift and brakes

You steer by pulling a brake toggle (which pulls down one side of the trailing edge) and by shifting your weight in the harness. A banked turn needs more lift: the load factor is n = 1 ÷ cos(bank), so 30° of bank needs 1.15 g, 45° needs 1.41 g and 60° needs 2 g. The USPPA's PPG2 novice rating limits flying to no more than 30° of bank.

A 45° banked turn: what load factor?

1 ÷ cos 45° ≈ 1.41 g.

CH 6

Wind, weather & the rules

Fly smooth air, always within glide of a field, and with the right permissions.

A paramotor flies at only roughly 30–45 km/h (illustrative), so wind matters a lot: a 25 km/h headwind can leave you barely moving over the ground. Engines can stop, so always fly with a landing area within gliding distance. Into the wind, your glide over the ground shrinks.

Worked example · engine-off glide from 300 m, 6 : 1 wing, 36 km/h airspeed (from our Thrust Lab)
WindGlide over the groundDistance from 300 m
Still air6 : 11.8 km
15 km/h headwind3.5 : 1≈ 1.05 km
15 km/h tailwind8.5 : 1≈ 2.55 km

300 × 6 × (36 − 15) ÷ 36 = 1,050 m.

What the safety guideline says

  • Smooth air: motorised flying needs smooth, laminar wind, so early morning and evening are generally suitable for training.
  • Tandem: not in more than 18 km/h of wind; no aerobatic manoeuvres below 600 ft; always fly with a landing zone within glide in case of engine failure.
  • Engines: instruments for cylinder-head temperature and RPM.
  • Permissions: an NOC through the district collector's office (with police clearance), Airports Authority of India clearance and a NOTAM giving the radius and height ceiling, then daily permission from local ATC.

The rules in India

NASG 2023 superseded the 2022 air-sports policy (confirmed in a July 2026 Lok Sabha reply). Press reports of Civil Aviation answers the same year describe ASFI as the NASG framework body, and list DGCA's safety/licensing remit for gliders, microlights, LSA, gyroplanes, balloons and powered hang gliders — not powered paragliders. The Version E5 safety guideline says PPG and powered parachutes are not regulated in India, so officially no licence is required, but pilots should keep a logbook and schools should certify level; it recommends BHPA, USPPA, APPI PPG or similar. ACI's organogram names a Microlight & Paramotor commissioner; that is not the same as a verified national PPG licence. Check the current position with the school, state tourism and airspace authorities before you fly.

From 300 m on a 6 : 1 wing at 36 km/h, with a 20 km/h headwind, how far can you glide over the ground?

300 × 6 × (36 − 20) ÷ 36 = 800 m.

04 · Governing bodies

Who sets the rules

Paramotor competitions come under the FAI; rating systems come from national associations abroad; in India, air sports sit under the Ministry of Civil Aviation's NASG 2023, with state safety guidelines for operators. We checked each on an official source.

Global

World federation

FAI Microlight and Paramotor Commission (CIMA)

The FAI commission for microlights and paramotors: world championships, records and competition rules. (Paragliding without an engine is under CIVL.)

fai.org
Rating system (reference)

United States Powered Paragliding Association (USPPA)

Publishes voluntary PPG1–PPG3 pilot ratings and tandem instructor requirements, based on the US FAA's Part 103 rules and advisory circular 103-7. Its public school directory is US-centred; we found no Indian school named there (checked 5 Oct 2026).

usppa.org
National association (UK)

British Hang Gliding & Paragliding Association (BHPA)

Runs a Powered Paragliding pathway (wing first, then motor, air law and navigation). BHPA Power qualifications are for solo flight only — no dual/passenger power qualification. Registered schools are listed on BHPA's site (UK-focused).

bhpa.co.uk

India

Ministry

Ministry of Civil Aviation · NASG 2023

A July 2026 Lok Sabha reply (Youth Affairs) confirms NASG 2023 superseded the National Air Sports Policy 2022. Separate Civil Aviation answers reported in the press (Aug 2026) describe NASG as the framework through ASFI. Read the primary documents; press summaries can blur which ministry answered what.

sansad.in (AU1311)
Regulator

Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)

As reported from the ministry's 2026 answers, DGCA continues to regulate safety and licensing for gliders, microlights, light sport aircraft, gyroplanes, balloons and powered hang gliders under the Aircraft Rules; powered paragliders were not on that list. Confirm the current CAR position before you rely on it.

dgca.gov.in
Safety guideline

Safety Guidelines for Paramotoring & powered parachutes (Version E5)

A Maharashtra-focused guideline; the same text appears in Maharashtra Tourism's air-activity safety guidelines. States PPG/PPC are not regulated so officially no licence is required, recommends BHPA/USPPA/APPI PPG-style training, logbooks and school-issued levels.

safetyguidelines.co.in

Aero Club of India · Microlight & Paramotor seat

ACI's published organogram includes a Commissioner for Microlight & Paramotor (and an ACI membership category for microlights & paramotors). The site mpci.in presents itself as that commission. We have not verified a public DGCA-recognised national PPG licence issued through it — treat school claims about MPCI licensing carefully.
